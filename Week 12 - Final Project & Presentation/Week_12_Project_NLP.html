Option B: NLP Sentiment Analyzer

Project: Text Analysis System

Goal: Build a Natural Language Processing model to automatically classify text based on emotion, sentiment (Positive/Negative/Neutral), or topic.

Specific Learning Outcomes
1. Master Text Preprocessing (Tokenization, Lemmatization, Stop-words).
2. Implement and Compare Sparse (TF-IDF) vs Dense (Embeddings) representations.
3. Train Sequence models (RNNs) or Dense Classifiers for text.

1. The Scenario

Social media feeds, product reviews, and customer support tickets generate massive amounts of unstructured text. Your task is to build an engine that reads this text and tags it. E.g., flagging "Urgent" support tickets or tracking the sentiment of a brand launch on Twitter.

2. Tools & Constraints

  • Dataset: IMDb Reviews, Amazon Products, Twitter Sentiment sets (Kaggle).
  • Libraries: NLTK/Spacy (preprocessing), TensorFlow/PyTorch (Modeling).
  • Constraint: You must implement Correction/Cleaning logic (handling emojis, slang, or URLs) specifically.

3. Step-by-Step Pipeline

Step 1: Text Preprocessing (The Heavy Lifting)

You cannot feed raw strings to a model.

  • Cleaning: Lowercasing, removing punctuation/HTML tags.
  • Tokenization: Splitting sentences into words.
  • Normalization: Lemmatization (running -> run) or Stemming.
  • Stop Word Removal: removing "the", "is", "at".

Step 2: Vectorization (The Core Requirement)

You must implement TWO approaches and compare them:

  1. Approach A (Traditional): Bag-of-Words or TF-IDF. (Sparse vectors).
  2. Approach B (Modern): Word Embeddings. Use a pre-trained embedding (GloVe/Word2Vec) OR learn an Embedding layer (Keras Embedding).

Step 3: Model Architecture

  • For Approach A: Logistic Regression or a simple Dense Neural Network.
  • For Approach B: An LSTM/GRU layer or 1D-CNN layer to capture context.

Step 4: Training & Evaluation

  • Compare the accuracy of Approach A vs Approach B.
  • Does the Embedding model understand "Not bad" (positive) better than the Bag-of-Words model?
  • Test Case: Create a list of 5 tricky sentences (sarcasm, double negatives) and see how your model predicts them.
