3. What is Clustering?

Definition: Grouping a set of objects so that objects in the same group (Cluster) are more similar to each other than to those in other groups.

  • Customer Segmentation: Grouping buyers by purchasing habits.

  • Image Compression: Grouping similar pixel colors.

  • Anomaly Detection: Finding points that don't belong to any group.

4. The K-Means Algorithm

The goal is to partition data into K clusters.

The Algorithm Loop:

  1. Initialize: Pick K random centroids.
  2. Assign: Color every point based on its nearest centroid.
  3. Update: Move the centroid to the average center of its points.
  4. Repeat: Keep cycling until centroids stop moving.

Think of the "Party Speaker" analogy. People move to the loudest speaker, then you move the speaker to the crowd's center. Repeat.

6. Picking "K" (The Elbow Method)

How do we know if there are 3 clusters or 5?

  • We calculate the Inertia (Tightness of clusters) for different K values.

  • The Elbow Rule: Look for the point where the improvement flattens out.


Adding more clusters after the elbow gives diminishing returns.
