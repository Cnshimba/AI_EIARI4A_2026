Artificial Intelligence Module - EIARI4A

Course structure

Course Overview

This course provides a comprehensive introduction to Artificial Intelligence, covering foundational mathematics, machine learning algorithms, neural networks, and modern AI applications including Generative AI and Large Language Models.

It is offered as an introductory module to AI in the Computer Systems Engineering program, as part of the Advanced Diploma curriculum. This serves as a foundation course to prepare students for more advanced modules in the Advanced Diploma and later the PGDip course.

Week 1: Introduction to Artificial Intelligence

Setting the Stage: From Turing to Transformers

Key Concepts

  • Artificial Intelligence (AI): The science of making machines perform tasks that typically require human intelligence (visual perception, speech recognition, decision-making).

  • Narrow AI vs. General AI:

  • Narrow AI (Weak): Systems designed for specific tasks (e.g., Siri, AlphaGo). Current state of the art.

  • General AI (Strong): Hypothetical machines with human-level cognitive abilities across any domain.

  • Schools of Thought:

  • Symbolism: Logic-based, rule-driven AI (e.g., Expert Systems). "Knowledge is represented by symbols."

  • Connectionism: Brain-inspired AI (e.g., Neural Networks). "Knowledge is distributed across weights."

  • The Turing Test: A measure of a machine's ability to exhibit intelligent behavior equivalent to, or indistinguishable from, that of a human.

Lecture Content

  1. The Birth of AI (1956): The Dartmouth Conference and the coining of the term by John McCarthy.
  2. Cycles of Hope and Despair: The "AI Winters" caused by overpromising and underdelivering, followed by the "Deep Learning Boom" (2012-Present).
  3. Modern AI Landscape:

  • Machine Learning (ML): Learning from data without explicit programming.

  • Deep Learning (DL): Neural networks with many layers, enabling feature extraction.

  • Generative AI: Creating new content (text, images) rather than just analyzing existing data.
    4. Impact on Society: Automation, bias, and the ethical responsibility of AI practitioners.

Learning Outcomes

By the end of this week, you will act as an "AI Historian" and "Futurist".

  • Understand foundational AI concepts and the difference between Narrow and General AI.

  • Identify key AI application areas and their impact on industry.

Activities

  • Read: Introductory chapters (Russell & Norvig, Chapter 1).
  • Watch: Video lectures on AI History.

Assessment

  • Short quiz on AI fundamentals.

Resources

  • The History of AI (ColdFusion)
  • What is Artificial Intelligence? (IBM)

Week 2: Mathematics for AI

The Engine Room: Linear Algebra and Probability

Key Concepts

  • Linear Algebra: The branch of mathematics concerning vector spaces and linear mappings between such spaces. It is the language of machine learning.

  • Vector: An object with magnitude and direction. Represents data points (e.g., [age, income]).

  • Matrix: A rectangular array of numbers. Represents datasets or transformations.

  • Dot Product: A measure of similarity between two vectors. Crucial for Neural Networks.

  • Probability Theory:

  • Conditional Probability: The likelihood of an event occurring given that another event has already occurred.

  • Bayes' Theorem: Describes the probability of an event, based on prior knowledge of conditions that might be related to the event. $P(A|B) = \frac{P(B|A)P(A)}{P(B)}$

  • Calculus in AI:

  • Gradient: The slope of a function. Points in the direction of steepest ascent. Used to optimize models (Gradient Descent).

Lecture Content

  1. Vectors and Matrices in Python: How NumPy handles arrays, shapes, and broadcasting. avoiding loops with Vectorization.
  2. Geometric Interpretation: Visualizing vectors as arrows, matrices as squishing/rotating space (Eigenvalues/Eigenvectors).
  3. Probability Distributions:
  • Gaussian (Normal) Distribution: The "Bell Curve" that appears everywhere in nature and data.
    4. From Math to Machines: How a neural network is essentially a series of Matrix Multiplications followed by non-linear functions.

Learning Outcomes

  • Apply linear algebra concepts (Dot Product, Matrix Multiplication) to ML models.

  • Understand probability for modelling uncertainty in data.

Activities

  • Python exercises with NumPy (Vectorization).
  • Math worksheets: Solving matrix operations by hand.

Assessment

  • Problem set: Matrix operations & Probability scenarios.

Resources

  • Essence of Linear Algebra (3Blue1Brown)
  • Probability Basics (StatQuest)
